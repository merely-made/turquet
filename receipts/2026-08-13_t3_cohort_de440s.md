validity and the kernel's coverage, sampling at 12:00 UTC. Residuals are
geocentric apparent ecliptic-of-date longitude and latitude in millidegrees,

## Skips, accounted

Every skipped sample is reported by reason rather than dropped silently.

- 90-day run: 4 skipped, all Pluto beyond its declared range, earliest at
  Julian year 2099.13.
- 7-day run: 53 skipped, all Pluto beyond its declared range, earliest at
  Julian year 2099.00.
- **Zero analytical failures and zero reference failures in both runs.**

The skips are the range guard working at the boundary, not a calculation
defect: the Pluto series is declared for 1885 to 2099 and refuses epochs past
it instead of degrading quietly.

## Committed vectors

`tests/vectors/cohort_de440s.tsv` holds 5,277 of the oracle's own values,
sampled at 149-day steps. The step is prime so the sampled epochs do not
alias with the year, the synodic month, or the sidereal month, giving a
modest file a wide spread of solar and lunar phase. Each row carries the TT
Julian day alongside a human-readable UTC label, and the TT value is the
authority, so the comparison measures position math rather than re-deriving a
time scale. The header records the kernel digest, the ANISE revision, the
frame, the units, and the command that regenerates it.

Three oracle samples were declined during emission because they fall outside
the analytical engine's declared Pluto range. A vector the engine states it
cannot serve is a contradiction rather than a test.

`tests/cohort_vectors.rs` reads that file in ordinary CI, with no kernel, no
network, and no optional feature. Worst residual against the committed set is
3 millidegrees, on the Moon; every other body holds 1 or better. The test
also asserts a floor on the number of vectors compared, so a truncated or
empty file cannot read as success, and asserts the header still names its
oracle, so a regenerated set cannot quietly lose provenance.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

Established: across 215 years and 112,137 comparisons, the kernel-free
analytical engine agrees with a DE440s-backed oracle to within 5 millidegrees
on geocentric apparent ecliptic coordinates, with no unexplained sample.

Not established by this run:

- topocentric results and high-latitude observers, which need the observer
  layer that does not exist yet;
- explicitly targeted planetary stations, lunar extremes, and eclipse
  instants; the regular grid samples broadly but does not seek those cases;
- SOFA conformance vectors for frame transformations, which belong to T2;
- equatorial output, deflection, and IAU 2006/2000A, which remain open in T3.

The regular-sampling portion of the evidence gate is met. The targeted-case
portion is not.
